Predictive Intelligence in R

CePH R Workshop Series 5 · Department of Social and Preventive Medicine, Universiti Malaya · 11–12 November 2026

Facilitator: Dr Vivek Jason Jayaraj · Venue: SPM Conference Room, Level 1, Block J, Faculty of Medicine, UM · Time: 9.00 am – 5.00 pm both days

Before you arrive: complete the setup (15 minutes, needs internet). Bring a laptop and charger.

What this workshop is

Two hands-on days that take you from “I can fit a logistic regression” to a complete, honest predictive-modelling workflow in R with tidymodels: a leak-free pipeline, four model families — random forests, support vector machines, XGBoost and neural networks — resampling and tuning, clinically meaningful evaluation and calibration, and model explanation. Every session has a self-guided tutorial that contains everything typed in the room, the outputs, the exercises and their solutions, so you can redo it at home.

Everything runs on one dataset — a 5,000-row synthetic NHMS-style survey — and one question — who has undiagnosed type 2 diabetes? — so that each new idea is a small change to code you already wrote.

Prerequisites

You should be comfortable reading and writing a dplyr pipeline and a ggplot. If not, the Refresher Course (4–5 November) covers exactly that. No prior machine-learning experience is assumed; no mathematics beyond “a weighted sum” and “a probability” is required — the neural-network session builds the rest from there.

The 2023 edition

This workshop grew out of the 2023 SBDR series (An Introduction to Machine Learning in R). The tidymodels pipeline, the StatQuest-derived illustrations and three of the four model families carry over. New in 2026: a synthetic dataset with real (and known) structure, the target-leakage thread, workflow_set() bake-offs, racing, calibration and threshold selection with probably, model-agnostic SHAP, a full neural-network session, one self-guided tutorial per session, and TRIPOD+AI.

Licence and use

Materials are for workshop use by CePH participants. Illustrations adapted from StatQuest (J. Starmer) and other sources are credited on the slides. The dataset is synthetic and may be reused freely for teaching.
