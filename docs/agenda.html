Agenda

Predictive Intelligence · CePH R Workshop Series 5 · 11–12 November 2026 · 9.00 am – 5.00 pm

How the two days are built

Eight sessions. Each one is a short lecture → live coding → “your turn”, and each has its own self-guided tutorial (menu above) that contains everything we typed, the outputs, the exercises and their solutions — so you can redo the session at home, or catch up if you fall behind in the room.

Four model families carry the workshop: random forests, support vector machines, XGBoost and neural networks. Logistic regression appears only as the baseline you already know. Day 1 builds the pipeline and the first two families; Day 2 adds the third, teaches you to validate, tune, evaluate and explain, and ends with neural networks.

Dataset for both days: data/nhms_synthetic.csv — 5,000 simulated Malaysian adults, NHMS-style variables. The question for both days: who has undiagnosed type 2 diabetes? (A take-home challenge on undiagnosed hypertension closes Tutorial 7.)

If you are running late

Every “Your turn” block is self-contained in its tutorial and can be finished at home. Protect the live coding and, on Day 2, protect the neural-network theory block — it is the content people came for and the hardest to self-teach.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the two days participants can:

  1. Distinguish a predictive from an explanatory model and choose the right validation design for a prediction question.
  2. Build a leak-free tidymodels pipeline: stratified split, recipe, workflow, resampling.
  3. Fit, compare and tune random forests, support vector machines, gradient-boosted trees and multilayer perceptrons, and explain in plain language how each one learns.
  4. Evaluate a classifier the way a clinician needs it evaluated — sensitivity, specificity and PPV at the real prevalence, ROC and PR curves, calibration, Brier score — and choose a defensible decision threshold.
  5. Explain a model globally (importance, partial dependence) and locally (SHAP), and state what those explanations do and do not mean.
  6. Describe what a neural network computes, how backpropagation trains it, and when it is the wrong tool.
  7. Report a model in line with TRIPOD+AI.
